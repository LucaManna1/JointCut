The geometry

A groove is a slice of a cutter, not a rectangle.

Most add-ins cut a box and call it a groove. JointCut models the volume the tool actually removes. A 7 mm cutter of 100.4 mm diameter, plunged to 14 mm, leaves a slot that breaks out 69.6 mm long on the jointing face and curves to a single deepest point in the middle.

That is why a 64 mm connector fits a groove which looks too long for it, and why the connector's own anchor edge is curved. Get it wrong in CAD and your shop drawing disagrees with the part that comes off the machine.

Set a travel distance and the same cutter is swept sideways, the way a CNC does it, stretching the groove with a flat bottom in the middle.

What it understands

Joints as they get modelled, not as they get specified.

  • Butt joints and T-joints
  • A shelf or divider landing in the middle of a panel face
  • Mitres, with the grooves mirrored about the mitre plane
  • Panels let into one another, machined from the face the panel enters through
  • Panels modelled slightly out of flush. Faces within 0.5 mm count as touching

It tells you before it cuts.

The foot of the dialog reads back what JointCut has found: joint type, joint length, both panel thicknesses. Warnings appear in the same place.

A panel too thin for the groove. Connectors spaced so closely the cuts would collide. A joint shorter than the connector. A lever hole that could never be reached by a hex key. Each one is a sentence you read before clicking Machine, not a failed feature you unpick afterwards.

Questions

Will the machining match my tooling?

JointCut models published machining data as faithfully as it can, but tooling, tolerances and shop practice vary. Check the geometry against your connector manufacturer's own processing data and make a test cut before committing material.

Can I change the dimensions?

Yes. Every figure lives in one plain-text file in millimetres, documented line by line. Edit it and click Run again. Nothing else in the add-in hard-codes a dimension.

Does it model the connectors themselves?

No. JointCut creates the machining only. The cuts are ordinary Fusion geometry and stay in your model if you later uninstall.

Is the machining associative?

No. Resize a panel after machining and the cuts stay where they were put. Delete them and run JointCut again.

Who do I ask when something goes wrong?

Me, directly. Include your Fusion version, the connector, and the text of any error message.
